# Playbook: saas-product

## Use when

Introducing or selling a software product to a buyer or daily user: launch video, landing-page hero, feature announcement. The viewer decides in under a minute whether this is for them. Not for step-by-step teaching (tutorial-walkthrough) or developer tooling (opensource-devtool).

## Viewer's question

"Is this my problem, and will this make my Monday easier?" They do not care what the product is called until the pain is recognised.

## Story core questions

- Whose one concrete day do we show (name, job, the hour it hurts)?
- What does the pain cost: hours, money, an embarrassing mistake?
- What does the viewer wrongly believe about the category ("accounting software is for big companies")?
- Which 2 or 3 product moments carry the promise? Everything else is cut.
- What proof is real: a number, a customer, a before/after clock?

## Beat skeleton

Pain, agitate, turn, show, proof, call to action. Apple's lesson: talk about the experience, not the spec. Studio motion practice adds empathy before the solution.

| Beat | % of runtime | Job | but / therefore link |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pain in one day | 15 | A named person at a specific hour, no product name | opens |
| Cost | 10 | The price of that pain, a number or a consequence | THEREFORE she stays late again |
| Turn | 8 | "Nhưng nếu..." the single idea | BUT it does not have to be so |
| Moment 1 | 18 | Product does the painful thing, in one visible action | THEREFORE the hour is gone |
| Moment 2 | 15 | Second moment, different angle (team, mobile, export) | BUT what about X? |
| Proof | 14 | Customer number, clock, before/after | THEREFORE it holds up |
| Call to action | 8 | One action, one URL, product name last | closes |
| Silence or end card | 12 | Hold the final frame long enough to read | n/a |

Example outline (Vietnamese, hoá đơn cho cửa hàng nhỏ):
1. Năm giờ chiều thứ Sáu, chị Lan còn bốn file Excel tên "final_v3".
2. Mỗi tháng chị mất mười một giờ chỉ để khớp hoá đơn.
3. Nhưng sếp cần số trước chín giờ sáng thứ Hai.
4. Vì vậy chị kéo thả ảnh hoá đơn vào một chỗ; mã số thuế sai tự hiện đỏ.
5. Mười một giờ còn ba mươi phút, đồng hồ trên màn hình chạy ngược.

## Hook types

- Pain in a number: "Mười một giờ mỗi tháng, chỉ để khớp hoá đơn."
- Misconception: "Phần mềm kế toán là của công ty lớn? Chị Lan có một mình ba cửa hàng."
- Scene: "Năm giờ chiều thứ Sáu. Bốn file Excel. Sếp cần số trước chín giờ sáng."

## Visual modes

- screen-cinema: the real product doing the task, cursor smooth, zoom to the click, branded background.
- match-cut-motif: one object (the crumpled invoice) changes state across scenes.
- split-compare: same task, same clock, old way against product.
- kinetic-type: the one number in the hook.

## Research needed

- Product facts from the owner: what it does, pricing, limits. Never invent a feature.
- A real customer, quote, and number with permission; otherwise say there is none and drop the proof beat.
- The pain in the buyer's words: support tickets, reviews, forum posts.
- Category misconception, found in real text.

## Traps

- Opening with the logo and name; the first three seconds belong to the pain.
- Feature tour: five features in five scenes with no person. Value-first test: delete all demo beats; the rest must still state the value.
- Zooming on every cursor move; zoom only on the click that matters, or the screen seems to shake.
- Stock "frustrated office worker" with a hand on the forehead; use the specific detail from research.
- Inventing a customer quote or a "save 80%" figure without a source.
- Ending with three calls to action (free trial, demo, newsletter); pick one.

## Review checklist

- [ ] A named person and a specific hour appear in the first 10 seconds.
- [ ] The product name is not spoken before the turn.
- [ ] Only 2 or 3 product moments, each one visible action.
- [ ] Every number has a source or is labelled as the customer's claim.
- [ ] One call to action.
- [ ] Remove demo beats: value still stated.

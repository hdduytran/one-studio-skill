# Playbook: history-timeline

## Use when

How something came to be over time: the origin of a product, a technology, a place, an event, a controversy. The viewer wants to understand why it is the way it is now.

## Viewer's question

"How did we get here, and what was not obvious back then?"

## Story core questions

- What is the unresolved tension that drives the story (not just the dates)?
- What did people at the time not know about the ending? Tell it from inside their ignorance (Ken Burns).
- Is the story one linear tale, or a contested one with several voices?
- Which cold open scene sets the central problem before the title?
- Which documents, photos or maps exist and are usable?

## Beat skeleton

Documentary uses fiction's rules: conflict and not knowing the outcome drive it. Chronology is the frame, but it may be bent. A cold open comes before the title. Linear suits a celebratory story; non-linear and multi-voice suits a disputed one.

| Beat | % of runtime | Job | but / therefore link |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cold open | 8 | One scene that poses the problem | opens |
| Starting world | 12 | How things were, with a telling detail | THEREFORE someone tried |
| First attempt | 15 | Who, what, why it seemed reasonable | BUT it failed |
| Turning event | 18 | The moment things shifted, dated | THEREFORE the stakes rose |
| Opposing voice | 15 | A rival or critic with their reasoning | BUT history chose differently |
| Consequence | 14 | What changed for ordinary people | THEREFORE today |
| What it explains now | 10 | Tie to the viewer's present | closes |
| Silence | 8 | Document or map held | n/a |

Example outline (Vietnamese, vì sao bàn phím QWERTY):
1. Năm 1873, một máy chữ kẹt phím mỗi khi gõ nhanh.
2. Nhưng ông Sholes không thể làm máy gõ chậm lại, nên ông đổi thứ tự phím.
3. Vì vậy các cặp chữ hay đi liền nhau bị tách xa nhau.
4. Nhiều người kể rằng chính sự "làm chậm" đó là mục đích; sử gia lại tranh cãi.
5. Bàn phím trên điện thoại của bạn vẫn giữ nguyên thứ tự đó.

## Hook types

- Cold open: "Năm 1873, ở Milwaukee, một chiếc máy chữ kẹt phím mỗi khi người gõ nhanh."
- Disputed claim: "Câu chuyện bạn nghe về bàn phím này có thể sai ở chỗ quan trọng nhất."
- Present link: "Ngón tay bạn đang gõ theo một lỗi của thế kỷ mười chín."

## Visual modes

- archive-documents: patents, letters, newspaper clippings with source and date; slow Ken Burns pan.
- world-camera: the timeline as one continuous world; camera travels along it.
- desk-collage: maps, paper cutouts, marker annotations for places and routes.
- kinetic-type: dates and one key quote.

## Research needed

- Primary sources first (letters, patents, minutes), then a historian's account; note where they disagree.
- Exact dates, places, names with spellings, checked in two sources.
- Image rights and provenance for every archive item.
- The popular myth and who debunked it.

## Traps

- A string of "and then in 1890" with no tension; every date beat must say what was at stake.
- Narrating with hindsight ("inevitably") and killing the not-knowing.
- Repeating a popular legend as fact; mark it as legend and show the evidence.
- Archive photos with no credit or date; viewers and rights holders both notice.
- Mixing names, places or dates between two versions of the story.
- Sixty years in one beat; cut a period rather than rush it.

## Review checklist

- [ ] A cold open before the first date.
- [ ] Tension named in one sentence.
- [ ] Each date beat connects by "but" or "therefore".
- [ ] Myths are labelled and checked.
- [ ] Every archive item has a source and a date.
- [ ] The present-day link is concrete.
